Drone Vision Architecture

How the Raspberry Pi companion finds an ArUco marker in the camera feed, takes the drone over from the pilot, and lands on it. Drawn from the modular src/ tree that replaced the single mavsdk_test.cpp.

10 modules · 19 source files · ≈1.9k lines C++17 2 threads of our own + MAVSDK and GStreamer threads 8 mission phases 47 config keys

The system in one breath

A drone carries a camera on a tilting gimbal. On a car roof there is a printed ArUco marker, ID 23. The companion computer watches the video, and when it sees the marker for five consecutive frames it switches PX4 into Offboard mode, flies the drone over the marker using velocities it derives from the marker's position in the frame, hovers for three seconds, descends, and hands PX4 the final landing. If the marker vanishes for 1.5 s or the pilot flips a mode switch, the companion lets go and PX4 holds position.

Everything in the design follows from one hard constraint of PX4 Offboard mode:

PX4 must receive a velocity setpoint every few tens of milliseconds, or it drops out of Offboard into a failsafe. Vision is slow and jittery. So vision and control run on separate threads, the control thread never waits on a frame, and every setpoint it sends is smoothed so a late frame never becomes a jerk.

Two deployments, one binary

The same executable runs in the Gazebo simulation and on the real aircraft. Only companion.conf changes: the MAVLink connection string and the video source. The figure shows what moves between the two.

One tick of the control loop

Every 20 ms the main thread runs the same eight steps. The sequence is the order in main.cpp, and the order matters: the pilot's velocity is seeded and the roller angle is read before the mission decides, so a takeover starts from the drone's current motion and the camera angle the pilot chose.

The mission state machine

Mission::update is pure logic. It takes the latest Measurement, the relative altitude and the time, and returns a Setpoint. Colour shows who is flying the aircraft in each phase.

From pixels to metres

One camera image cannot give distance by itself. The companion gets it from a right triangle: the drone knows its height, the gimbal knows its pitch, and the marker's pixel offset gives an angle. The tracker converts pixels to angles with a pinhole model, and the mission converts angles plus height into metres forward and to the right.

Who points the camera

The gimbal never decides anything about the mission. Its only job is to keep the marker in frame, and its only output that matters is the angle the geometry trusts. Four sources compete for the target angle, one per situation, and one chain decides what angle to believe.

Configuration precedence

Four layers, each overriding the one before it:

defaults in struct Config   →   companion.conf next to the binary   →   --config=FILE   →   --key=value on the CLI

The remote replaced the long if-else chain in applyKV with a lookup table from key name to a pointer to member of Config, wrapped in a std::variant over the five field types. std::visit then dispatches on the actual type and calls stoi, stof, stol, parseBool or a plain string copy. Adding a parameter is now one field in the struct and one line in the table. Two old key names, stable_time_ms and pitch_init_deg, are kept as aliases.

Bench mode instead of flight

--selftest=gimbal|rc|all makes main() connect, initialise the gimbal and RC input, then call runSelfTest and exit. Nothing arms and Offboard is never started. run_companion.sh notices the flag and skips the network and video checks, because on the bench there is no laptop to ping.

selftest=gimbal

Does the camera do what the code believes?

Checks that PX4 reports a gimbal angle at all. Drives the camera to pitch_max then pitch_min, timing each travel. Flags a reversed axis, a range the servo cannot reach, and a gimbal_gain that would command faster than the measured speed. Prints the gimbal_rate_deg_s to write into the config. Without feedback it runs the camera back and forth and asks you for a stopwatch.

selftest=rc

Is it the right channel, and where are its stops?

For 20 s it mirrors the roller onto the camera and records the minimum and maximum pulse width seen. Flags a channel that never moves or travels less than 300 µs, and prints the rc_pitch_min_us and rc_pitch_max_us values to use. Tells you whether to set rc_pitch_reversed.

exit codes

0 · 2 · 1

Zero means everything agreed. Two means the test ran and printed a numbered list of things to fix. One means it could not start, for example because the vehicle is armed, which it refuses outright.

The failsafe layer above the mission

PX4 runs its own failsafes in parallel and they outrank the mission. The companion sees them only as a flight-mode change in telemetry and must accept them. The one that matters most for this architecture is what PX4 does when the companion stops sending setpoints.

  • Companion dies or hangs. No setpoint for COM_OF_LOSS_T and PX4 takes the Offboard-loss action, which must be Hold or Return and never Manual. The docs call this the single most important parameter and ask for a live test: kill the process in the air and watch PX4 recover.
  • Pilot moves the mode switch. PX4 leaves Offboard. The companion sees flight_mode != Offboard, calls abort(), and stays out for 15 s.
  • RC or ground-station link lost, low battery, GPS lost, geofence. PX4's own Return, Land or Hold actions take over. The companion does not read battery state, which the audit lists as a finding.
  • Video link to the operator lost. No effect on control on the real aircraft, because detection runs on the Pi before encoding. In simulation this is the opposite: the video is the input, and losing it zeroes the velocities within 500 ms.

Where the docs and the code disagree

Local CLAUDE.md is stale. It describes a flat repo with one source file. After pulling, the layout caveat and the single-file architecture section need rewriting.

docs/diagrams.md draws the target video path, not the implemented one. Its data-flow diagram shows libcamerasrc, a tee before the encoder, and the Y plane of NV12 fed straight to the detector. src/video.cpp still opens a local camera with v4l2src as MJPEG 720p and converts to BGR, then the tracker converts back to grey. The audit's findings 6 to 9 acknowledge this and the README lists the libcamerasrc branch as one of three things to do before the first autonomous flight.

.DS_Store files were committed at the repo root and in docs/. The new .gitignore adds them, but the two already tracked need git rm --cached.
